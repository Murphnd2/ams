What Is an FSA?

A Flexible Spending Account (FSA) is a tax-advantaged benefit that allows employees to set aside pre-tax dollars to pay for eligible healthcare expenses. By reducing taxable income, participants can save significantly on out-of-pocket medical costs throughout the plan year.

Eligible Expenses

  • Doctor and specialist copays
  • Prescription medications
  • Dental treatments and orthodontia
  • Vision exams, glasses, and contacts
  • Physical therapy and chiropractic care
  • Mental health counseling
  • Medical equipment and supplies
  • Over-the-counter medications

How to Enroll

Employees may enroll during their employer's annual open enrollment period or within 30 days of a qualifying life event. Once enrolled, participants select their annual contribution amount, and pre-tax deductions are taken from each paycheck throughout the plan year.

Debit Card Access: Enrolled participants receive an FSA debit card for instant access to their account balance at the point of sale. No need to submit paper claims for most eligible purchases.

Contribution Limits

The IRS sets annual contribution limits for Health FSAs. For the current plan year, employees may contribute up to the maximum allowed amount. Your employer may also offer a grace period or carryover provision to help manage unused balances at year-end.

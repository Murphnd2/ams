# BUSINESS ASSOCIATE AGREEMENT
Document reference: **BAA-2026**

This Business Associate Agreement (this "BAA") is entered into as of ______________ by and between **______________________________________**, acting as plan sponsor on behalf of the group health plan or plans it maintains (collectively, "Covered Entity"), and **Superior State Administrators, Inc.**, a Michigan corporation formerly doing business as Superior State Employer Solutions ("SSA") (each a "Party" and together the "Parties").

Covered Entity has entered into a Master Services Agreement with SSA for third party administration and related services (the "Services Agreement"), to which this BAA is attached and into which it is incorporated. SSA acknowledges that it is a **Business Associate** of Covered Entity as those terms are defined by the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act and its implementing regulations, 45 C.F.R. Parts 160–164 ("HIPAA").

In consideration of the mutual covenants and conditions contained here, the Parties agree as follows.

---

## 1. Definitions

Capitalized terms not otherwise defined here have the meanings set forth in HIPAA and in the Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health Act of 2009 ("HITECH Act"), which definitions are incorporated by reference. References to a section of HIPAA mean that section as in effect or as amended.

---

## 2. Obligations and activities of SSA

**2.1 Permitted use.** SSA will use or disclose Protected Health Information ("PHI") received from or on behalf of Covered Entity, or created for Covered Entity, only as permitted or required by this BAA, as required by law, or for SSA's own management, administration, and legal and compliance purposes as provided in Section 3.

**2.2 Privacy safeguards.** SSA will develop, implement, maintain, and use appropriate administrative, technical, and physical safeguards to protect the privacy of PHI. The safeguards will reasonably protect PHI from intentional or unintentional use or disclosure in violation of the Privacy Rule and limit incidental uses and disclosures made pursuant to a use or disclosure otherwise permitted by this BAA.

**2.3 Security Rule.** SSA will comply with the Security Rule and will use appropriate administrative, technical, and physical safeguards that reasonably and appropriately protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of Electronic PHI that SSA creates, receives, maintains, or transmits on Covered Entity's behalf, including the technologies and methodologies specified by the Secretary for rendering Electronic PHI unusable, unreadable, or indecipherable to unauthorized individuals.

**2.4 Mitigation.** SSA will mitigate, to the extent practicable, any harmful effect known to SSA of a use or disclosure of PHI by SSA in violation of this BAA.

**2.5 Reporting non-permitted use or disclosure.** SSA will report to Covered Entity any use or disclosure of PHI not provided for by this BAA of which it becomes aware, **without unreasonable delay and no later than ten (10) business days** after discovery.

**2.6 Security incidents.** SSA will report Security Incidents affecting Electronic PHI as provided in Sections 2.5 and 2.7. **The Parties agree that unsuccessful attempts to access, use, disclose, modify, or destroy Electronic PHI or to interfere with system operations — including pings and other broadcast attacks, port scans, unsuccessful log-on attempts, denial of service attacks, and malware not resulting in unauthorized access — do not require individual reporting, and that this Section constitutes notice of them.** SSA will furnish a summary of such activity on Covered Entity's reasonable written request, no more than annually.

**2.7 Breach notification.** SSA will notify Covered Entity of any Breach of Unsecured PHI **without unreasonable delay and no later than fifteen (15) calendar days** after discovery of the Breach by SSA, unless a delay is required or permitted under applicable law or by a law enforcement official. SSA will treat a Breach as discovered in accordance with 45 C.F.R. §164.410. The notification will include, to the extent then known: (i) a brief description of the Breach, including the date of the Breach and the date of discovery; (ii) the types of PHI involved; (iii) the identity of each individual whose PHI was involved, to the extent known; (iv) who made the non-permitted use or disclosure and who received it; (v) corrective action taken; and (vi) such other information, including a written report and the risk assessment under 45 C.F.R. §164.402, as Covered Entity may reasonably request. SSA will supplement its notification as additional information becomes available.

**2.8 Subcontractors and agents.** SSA will ensure that any agent or subcontractor to which it provides PHI received from, or created or received by SSA on behalf of, Covered Entity agrees in writing to restrictions and conditions no less protective than those that apply to SSA under this BAA, and agrees to implement reasonable and appropriate safeguards to protect PHI.

**2.9 Access.** At Covered Entity's written request, and in the time and manner mutually agreed or designated by the Secretary, SSA will provide access to PHI in a Designated Record Set in SSA's custody or control, to Covered Entity or, as Covered Entity directs, to the Individual or the Individual's designee, to meet the requirements of 45 C.F.R. §164.524. Where Covered Entity requests an electronic copy of PHI maintained electronically, SSA will provide it in the form and format specified if readily producible; if not readily producible, SSA will work with Covered Entity to determine an alternative form and format that enables Covered Entity to meet its electronic access obligations.

**2.10 Amendment.** SSA will make any amendment to PHI in a Designated Record Set that Covered Entity directs or agrees to pursuant to 45 C.F.R. §164.526, at the request of Covered Entity or an Individual, in the time and manner mutually agreed or designated by the Secretary.

**2.11 No sale of PHI.** SSA will not receive, directly or indirectly, remuneration in exchange for any PHI of an Individual unless Covered Entity has received a valid authorization from the Individual or an exception under HIPAA or the HITECH Act applies. **De-identified data created under Section 3.6 is not PHI, and nothing in this Section limits SSA's rights under that Section.**

**2.12 Books and records.** SSA will make its internal practices, books, and records, including policies and procedures and PHI, relating to the use and disclosure of PHI received from or created or received on behalf of Covered Entity, available to Covered Entity or to the Secretary, in a time and manner mutually agreed or designated by the Secretary, for purposes of the Secretary determining Covered Entity's compliance with the Privacy and Security Rules.

**2.13 Accounting of disclosures.** SSA will document disclosures of PHI and information related to those disclosures as would be required for Covered Entity to respond to a request by an Individual for an accounting of disclosures under 45 C.F.R. §164.528, and will provide that information to Covered Entity or, as Covered Entity directs, to an Individual, in a time and manner mutually acceptable to the Parties.

**2.14 Standard transactions.** If SSA transmits or receives any Covered Electronic Transactions on behalf of Covered Entity, it will comply with the applicable provisions of the Standards for Electronic Transactions Rule to the extent required by law, and will require any agent assisting it to agree in writing to do the same.

**2.15 Minimum necessary.** SSA will limit its requests for, uses of, and disclosures of PHI to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose, consistent with 45 C.F.R. §164.502(b).

**2.16 Disclosure to the plan sponsor.** ⚠️ **SSA will not disclose PHI to Covered Entity in its capacity as plan sponsor except (a) to the individuals identified on the Designated Contact List (SCH-CONTACT) to the Services Agreement, (b) as permitted by 45 C.F.R. §164.504(f) and the Plan's documents, and (c) as summary health information or enrollment and disenrollment information as permitted by that section.** SSA will not use or disclose PHI for employment-related actions or decisions, or in connection with any other benefit or employee benefit plan, and Covered Entity will not request it to do so.

## 3. Permitted uses and disclosures by SSA

Except as otherwise limited in this BAA, SSA may use or disclose PHI to perform functions, activities, or services for or on behalf of Covered Entity as specified in the Services Agreement, provided that the use or disclosure would not violate the Privacy or Security Rules if done by Covered Entity, and subject to the minimum necessary requirement.

**3.1** SSA may use PHI for its own proper management and administration and to carry out its legal responsibilities.

**3.2** SSA may use PHI to provide administrative services to Covered Entity as permitted by 45 C.F.R. §164.504(e)(2)(i)(A).

**3.3** SSA may disclose PHI for its own proper management and administration, provided the disclosure is Required by Law, or SSA obtains reasonable assurances from the recipient that the information will remain confidential and be used or further disclosed only as Required by Law or for the purpose for which it was disclosed, and that the recipient will notify SSA of any breach of confidentiality of which it becomes aware.

**3.4** SSA may use PHI to provide Data Aggregation services to Covered Entity as permitted by 45 C.F.R. §164.504(e)(2)(i)(B).

**3.5** SSA may use PHI to report violations of law to appropriate federal and state authorities, consistent with 45 C.F.R. §164.502(j)(1).

**3.6 De-identification.** SSA may de-identify PHI in accordance with 45 C.F.R. §164.514(a)–(b) and may use and disclose the resulting de-identified and aggregated data as provided in the Services Agreement. Such data is not PHI and is not subject to this BAA.

## 4. Obligations of Covered Entity

**4.1** On request, Covered Entity will provide SSA with a copy of its Notice of Privacy Practices, and will notify SSA of any limitation in that Notice under 45 C.F.R. §164.520 to the extent the limitation may affect SSA's use or disclosure of PHI.

**4.2** Covered Entity will notify SSA of any change in, or revocation of, permission by an Individual to use or disclose PHI, to the extent the change may affect SSA's use or disclosure of PHI.

**4.3** Covered Entity will notify SSA of any restriction on the use or disclosure of PHI that it has agreed to under 45 C.F.R. §164.522, to the extent the restriction may affect SSA's use or disclosure of PHI. **SSA is not bound by a restriction of which it has not been given written notice.**

**4.4** Covered Entity will maintain Plan documents that permit the disclosures described in Section 2.16 and will maintain an accurate Designated Contact List.

---

## 5. Permissible requests by Covered Entity

Except as otherwise permitted by this BAA, Covered Entity will not request SSA to use or disclose PHI in any manner that would not be permissible under the Privacy or Security Rules if done by Covered Entity, except that SSA may use or disclose PHI for Data Aggregation or for its own management and administrative activities as provided in Section 3 and in the Services Agreement.

## 6. Term and termination

**6.1 Term.** This BAA is effective on execution by both Parties and is **co-terminous with the Services Agreement**, continuing thereafter until all PHI provided by Covered Entity to SSA, or created or received by SSA on behalf of Covered Entity, is returned, destroyed, or protected as provided in Section 6.3.

**6.2 Termination for cause.** On Covered Entity's knowledge of a material breach of this BAA by SSA, Covered Entity will either (a) provide SSA a reasonable opportunity to cure the breach or end the violation and terminate this BAA if SSA does not cure within the time specified; (b) immediately terminate this BAA if SSA has breached a material term and cure is not possible; or (c) if neither termination nor cure is feasible, report the violation to the Secretary.

**6.3 Effect of termination.**
**(a)** On termination for any reason, SSA will return or destroy all PHI received from Covered Entity, or created or received by SSA on behalf of Covered Entity, including PHI in the possession of its subcontractors or agents, and will retain no copies — **except that SSA may retain PHI to the extent required by applicable law or by its legal, tax, audit, insurance, or recordkeeping obligations, including its record retention obligation under the Services Agreement.** SSA will extend the protections of this BAA to any PHI so retained, and limit further uses and disclosures of it to the purposes that make its retention necessary, for as long as it is retained.
**(b)** If SSA determines that returning or destroying PHI is infeasible, SSA will notify Covered Entity of the conditions that make it infeasible, extend the protections of this BAA to that PHI, and limit further uses and disclosures accordingly, for as long as SSA maintains it.

## 7. Legal actions, indemnity, and liability

**7.1 Subpoenas and legal process.** If either Party receives a subpoena or similar notice, request, or legal process from any judicial, administrative, or other body arising out of or in connection with this BAA — including any unauthorized use or disclosure of PHI or any failure in security measures — **the receiving Party will promptly notify the other Party, to the extent legally permitted, and afford it a reasonable opportunity to participate in the response.** The Party whose information is sought may seek a protective order or other relief at its own cost. Each Party will reasonably cooperate with the other in responding.

**7.2 Indemnification.**
**(a)** SSA will indemnify, defend, and hold harmless Covered Entity and its affiliates, trustees, officers, directors, employees, and agents from and against any claim, cause of action, liability, damage, cost, or expense, including reasonable attorneys' fees and court or proceeding costs, arising out of or in connection with any unauthorized use or disclosure of PHI, any failure in security measures affecting PHI, or any other breach of this BAA **by SSA or by any person or entity under SSA's control, including its subcontractors and agents.**
**(b)** Covered Entity will indemnify, defend, and hold harmless SSA and its affiliates, trustees, officers, directors, employees, and agents from and against any claim, cause of action, liability, damage, cost, or expense, including reasonable attorneys' fees and court or proceeding costs, arising out of or in connection with any unauthorized use or disclosure of PHI, any failure in security measures affecting PHI, or any other breach of this BAA **by Covered Entity or by any person or entity under Covered Entity's control, including its subcontractors, agents, and its broker or agent of record.**

**7.3 Indemnity procedure.** The indemnity procedure set forth in the Services Agreement governs all claims under Section 7.2, including notice, control of the defense with counsel of the indemnifying Party's choice, cooperation, and the requirement that neither Party settle a claim in a manner admitting liability on behalf of, or imposing a non-monetary obligation on, the other Party without that Party's prior written consent.

**7.4 Breach costs.** As between the Parties, the Party whose breach of this BAA caused a Breach of Unsecured PHI bears the direct costs of notification required under 45 C.F.R. §§164.404–164.410 and applicable state law, including notice preparation and delivery, call center support, regulatory response, and `[credit monitoring where required or reasonably necessary]`, subject to Section 7.5.

**7.5 Limitation of liability.** ⚠️ **The limitation of liability set forth in the Services Agreement applies to this BAA and to all claims arising out of or relating to it, including claims under Section 7.2 and costs under Section 7.4.** `[Optional super-cap, if negotiated: notwithstanding the foregoing, SSA's aggregate liability for costs under Section 7.4 arising from SSA's breach of this BAA will not exceed the greater of the cap stated in the Services Agreement or ___, and in no event will exceed the limits of SSA's applicable insurance.]`

## 8. General provisions

**8.1 Regulatory references.** A reference to a section of the Privacy or Security Rules means that section as in effect or as amended.

**8.2 Amendment.** The Parties will take such action as is necessary to amend this BAA from time to time as is necessary for Covered Entity to comply with the requirements of HIPAA, the HITECH Act, and the Privacy and Security Rules.

**8.3 Survival.** SSA's obligations under Sections 2, 3, 6.3, and 7 survive termination of this BAA.

**8.4 Interpretation.** Any ambiguity in this BAA will be resolved to permit Covered Entity to comply with the Privacy and Security Rules.

**8.5 No third-party beneficiaries.** Nothing in this BAA confers any right, remedy, obligation, or liability on any person other than the Parties and their respective successors and permitted assigns.

**8.6 Conflicts of law.** To the extent the law of the state in which Covered Entity does business is more stringent than federal law regarding privacy, that state law controls unless preempted. **Each Party will comply with applicable state data breach notification law with respect to information in its possession.**

**8.7 Relationship to the Services Agreement.** This BAA controls as to the use and disclosure of PHI. The Services Agreement controls as to all other matters, including limitation of liability, indemnity procedure, term, termination, dispute resolution, and governing law.

**8.8 Counterparts; electronic execution.** This BAA may be executed in counterparts, including electronic and imaged counterparts, each of which is an original and all of which constitute one agreement. Electronic signatures have the same force and effect as manual signatures.

## Notes to Kevin

### The six fixes, and where they landed

| Problem in your current BAA | Fix |
|---|---|
| §7.2.1 uncapped indemnity, §7.3 duty to fund the client's chosen counsel, §7.4 client controls settlement while SSA still indemnifies | **§7.5** subjects the whole article to the MSA's cap; **§7.3** replaces the tender-of-defense mechanics with the MSA's symmetric procedure; the old §7.3 and §7.4 are **deleted** |
| §7.1 garbled ("SSA receives a subpoena … CLIENT shall forward a copy of such subpoena CLIENT") | **§7.1** rewritten as a reciprocal notify-and-cooperate clause |
| §6.3.1 "SSA shall retain no copies" conflicting with the retention obligation | **§6.3(a)** retention carve-out, cross-referenced to the MSA |
| §2.6 monthly reports of unsuccessful attempts "including pings," by the 10th | **§2.6** deems trivial incidents reported, with an annual summary on request |
| §2.5 and §2.7 both at 30 days, consuming half the covered entity's 60-day clock | **§2.5** ten business days, **§2.7** fifteen calendar days |
| Breach costs unallocated | **§7.4** allocates to the breaching Party, subject to the cap |

### Added, not in your current BAA

1. **§2.16 — disclosure to the plan sponsor.** The most substantive addition. It ties PHI disclosure to the Designated Contact List and to 45 C.F.R. §164.504(f), and bars use for employment-related decisions. It protects you: without it, every disclosure you make to an employer contact rests on the employer's own compliance rather than on a written limit you can point to.
2. **§2.15 minimum necessary**, **§3.6 de-identification** (paired with §2.11 so the aggregate-data right doesn't read as a sale of PHI), **§4.3** last sentence (not bound by unnotified restrictions), **§4.4** (plan documents must permit the §2.16 disclosures), **§8.6** second sentence, **§8.7** precedence, **§8.8** electronic execution.

### Decisions open

1. **§7.5 super-cap.** Bracketed. Same recommendation as the MSA: have it drafted before a client's counsel asks.
2. **§7.4 credit monitoring.** Bracketed. "Where required or reasonably necessary" is the middle position; a flat commitment is expensive and a flat refusal is hard to defend.
3. **§2.5 and §2.7 timing.** Set at what a sophisticated employer will accept. ⚠️ **Only you can confirm SSA can actually perform at ten business days and fifteen calendar days.** An unmeetable deadline is a stipulated breach — the same defect the ping-report clause had.

### Flags

1. ⚠️ **The covered entity is the group health plan, not the employer.** I drafted the parties clause as the employer "acting as plan sponsor on behalf of the group health plan or plans it maintains," which is the accurate framing and is how §2.16 becomes coherent. Your current BAA names the employer flatly as "CLIENT." The change is correct but it is a change, and an employer's counsel may ask about it.
2. ⚠️ **§7.5 is the clause a client's counsel is most likely to strike.** If it goes, finding #2 of the original review reopens in full — the BAA indemnity becomes uncapped again. It is worth knowing in advance what you will and won't trade for it.
3. **Which clients need a BAA at all.** A POP-only or transit-only engagement involves no PHI. The MSA's structure makes the BAA a separate document, so it can be omitted — but my recommendation stays: include it in every envelope, because the cost is zero once §7.5 holds and the scoping argument is one you'd rather not have later.

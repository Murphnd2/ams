# INDIVIDUAL COVERAGE HRA RIDER
**Superior State Administrators, Inc. ("SSA")**
Rider reference: **RIDER-ICHRA**

Employer has established or will establish an **individual coverage health reimbursement arrangement** ("ICHRA") under Internal Revenue Code §105 and 26 C.F.R. §54.9802-4, and has elected ICHRA administration services in Article VII of the Master Services Agreement (the "Agreement").

This Rider is incorporated into and made a part of the Agreement and supplements the **Reimbursement Account Administration Appendix (APX-RA)**. Sections A through F of APX-RA apply to the ICHRA except as this Rider provides otherwise.

**Order of Precedence.** As to any ICHRA-specific matter, this Rider controls over APX-RA. As to the scope and performance of reimbursement account services generally, APX-RA controls. The Agreement controls as to all other matters, including limitation of liability, indemnification, term, termination, and governing law. The Business Associate Agreement controls as to the use and disclosure of Protected Health Information, subject to the Agreement's limitation of liability, which applies to it.

---

## A. Services SSA provides

In addition to the services described in APX-RA, SSA will:

**A.1 Documents.** Prepare the text of initial drafts of the ICHRA plan document and summary plan description, and — where a cafeteria plan rail is elected under Section E — of the cafeteria plan document or amendment. Drafts are furnished for Employer's review with its own legal counsel; adoption and execution are Employer's alone.

**A.2 Participant notice.** Prepare the notice required to be furnished to employees eligible for the ICHRA, containing the elements required by 26 C.F.R. §54.9802-4(c)(6), and deliver it through SSA's platform or by mail as Employer directs, **on the schedule Employer establishes under Section C.**

**A.3 Opt-out and waiver.** Capture, date, and retain each participant's election to opt out of and waive future reimbursement from the ICHRA, and each revocation where the Plan's terms and applicable law permit one, and make the resulting status available to Employer.

**A.4 Substantiation.** Administer the substantiation program described in Section D.

**A.5 Reimbursement and premium payment.** Process reimbursement of, or direct payment of, substantiated individual health insurance premiums and other eligible expenses to the extent the ICHRA's terms provide, by the funding methods elected under APX-RA Section C, including the electronic payment card under APX-CARD.

**A.6 Testing.** Conduct §105(h) nondiscrimination testing as provided in APX-RA Section A.10, on the data Employer certifies.

**A.7 Records.** Maintain records of eligibility, class assignment as Employer designates it, notice delivery, opt-out and waiver elections, substantiation, and reimbursement activity.

**A.8 Affordability computation.** `[Decision — in scope or out at launch; see notes.]` Where elected, furnish Employer a computation bearing on the affordability of the ICHRA offer, based on data Employer provides and on third-party data sources. **Any such computation is informational, is not a determination, is furnished to Employer and, at Employer's direction, to Employer's licensed broker or agent of record, and is not furnished to any employee.** Section 5.9 of the Agreement applies.

**A.9 Renewal.** Administer annual renewal, including re-solicitation of substantiation and opt-out elections for the new Plan Year.

## B. Determinations reserved to Employer

Employer makes each of the following determinations. **SSA implements what Employer determines, relies on it under Section 5.2 of the Agreement, and makes no determination of its own:**

**B.1 Classes and contributions.** The classes of employees to whom the ICHRA is offered, whether the permitted classes are used and how they are defined, the contribution amount for each class, and whether amounts vary by age or by number of dependents.

**B.2 Minimum class size.** Whether a minimum class size requirement applies to any class and whether it is satisfied.

**B.3 Same terms.** Whether the ICHRA is offered on the same terms to all employees within a class.

**B.4 No other group coverage.** That no employee offered the ICHRA is also offered a traditional group health plan by Employer for the same Plan Year, as required by 26 C.F.R. §54.9802-4.

**B.5 ALE status and affordability.** Whether Employer is an applicable large employer, and any determination as to whether the ICHRA offer is affordable for purposes of Code §4980H(b). **Employer makes this determination. SSA's computation under Section A.8, if any, is an input Employer may consider and is not the determination.**

**B.6 Reporting.** The applicability of and compliance with Code §6055 and §6056 reporting, Form 720 (PCORI), Form 5500, and Form W-2 reporting, as allocated in Section 2.3 of the Agreement and APX-RA Section B.9.

**B.7 ERISA safe harbor conditions.** Whether the conditions of the safe harbor at 26 C.F.R. §54.9802-4(f) — under which individual health insurance coverage reimbursed by the ICHRA is not treated as part of an ERISA plan — are satisfied. **Employer will:**
- furnish participants the annual notice that the individual coverage is not subject to ERISA;
- **not endorse, select, recommend, or otherwise direct employees toward any particular issuer, plan, or policy;**
- **not offer any cash or other taxable benefit as an alternative to the ICHRA** outside the ICHRA's own terms;
- **receive no cash, fee, or other consideration** in connection with any employee's individual coverage; and
- ensure that enrollment in individual coverage is voluntary and not a term or condition of employment.

**SSA's services are designed to support these conditions, but Employer's own conduct determines whether they are met, and Employer is responsible for that conduct under Article V of the Agreement.**

**B.8 Funding.** Funding of the ICHRA and reimbursement of amounts settled in advance of collection, as provided in APX-PAYMENT and APX-CARD.

**B.9 Continuation premium.** The applicable premium for continuation of the ICHRA, on an actuarially reasonable basis, as provided in APX-COBRA Section D.6.

## C. Notice timing

⚠️ **Employer establishes the ICHRA's plan year, offer dates, and coverage effective dates, and therefore establishes the schedule on which the participant notice must be furnished.** SSA prepares and delivers the notice on the schedule Employer establishes. **SSA does not determine, compute, or represent the date by which the notice is required to be furnished**, and no date is stated in this Rider. Employer is responsible for determining the applicable deadline, including for a newly established ICHRA and for an employee who becomes eligible after the beginning of a Plan Year, in consultation with its own counsel.

Employer will furnish SSA the data necessary to prepare and deliver each notice sufficiently in advance of Employer's schedule for SSA to perform. **Section 5.3 of the Agreement applies: SSA's delivery obligation runs from receipt of complete and accurate data.**

## D. Substantiation

**D.1 Structure.** The ICHRA's substantiation program requires, in accordance with 26 C.F.R. §54.9802-4(c)(5) and the ICHRA's terms:
**(a)** substantiation that the participant and each covered dependent is or will be enrolled in individual health insurance coverage for the Plan Year, provided at or before the first reimbursement of the Plan Year; and
**(b)** substantiation, accompanying or with respect to each request for reimbursement, that the individual remains enrolled in such coverage for the month to which the request relates.

**D.2 Attestation.** Substantiation under D.1 may be satisfied by the participant's written attestation in the form SSA administers, unless the ICHRA's terms require documentation from a third party. **SSA may rely on a participant's attestation unless SSA has actual knowledge that it is false.**

**D.3 Channel.** Where the ICHRA's terms or the cafeteria plan rail under Section E require that the individual coverage be purchased outside an Exchange, the attestation establishes both enrollment and that the coverage was not purchased through an Exchange.

**D.4 Effect of non-substantiation.** Where substantiation for a period is not provided, **reimbursement for that period is held** and is released if and when substantiation is provided, subject to the ICHRA's claim submission deadlines. A failure to substantiate is not by itself a termination of participation.

**D.5 Contradictory information.** ⚠️ Where SSA receives information indicating that a participant's individual coverage has terminated or lapsed for a period, **SSA will hold reimbursement for the affected period and refer the matter to Employer**, notwithstanding any attestation covering that period.

**D.6 Records.** Attestation records are retained as provided in Section 3.5 of the Agreement, identify the version of the attestation text to which the participant responded, and are not modified after capture.

## E. Cafeteria plan salary reduction — off-Exchange coverage only

**This Section applies only where Employer elects a cafeteria plan rail alongside the ICHRA.**

**E.1 Scope of the rail.** Salary reduction under Employer's cafeteria plan is available for individual health insurance coverage **only where that coverage is purchased outside an Exchange**, and **only with respect to a participant who is covered by the ICHRA.** Employer acknowledges that Code §125(f)(3) prohibits a qualified health plan offered through an Exchange from being a qualified benefit under a cafeteria plan.

**E.2 Amount.** A participant's salary reduction election for individual coverage is limited to the premium net of the ICHRA amount available to that participant, and is reset when the premium or the ICHRA amount changes. Elections are prospective only.

**E.3 Participant attestation.** Each participant electing salary reduction for individual coverage attests that the coverage was purchased outside an Exchange and that the participant is covered by the ICHRA, and agrees to notify SSA or Employer if either ceases to be true.

**E.4 Change in status.** A participant who obtains coverage through an Exchange, or who opts out of and waives the ICHRA, **ceases to be eligible for salary reduction for individual health insurance coverage for the remainder of the Plan Year, and the election terminates prospectively.** Employer is responsible for the payroll consequences of the termination.

**E.5 No facilitation of Exchange enrollment.** Neither SSA nor, under Section B.7, Employer facilitates, assists with, or directs any employee toward enrollment through an Exchange. An employee remains free to obtain Exchange coverage; **Employer neither prevents nor assists it.**

**E.6 Excepted benefit premiums — separate basis.** Premiums for accident-only, specified disease, and other excepted benefit insurance may be a qualified benefit under a cafeteria plan **on their own footing, independent of the ICHRA and of this Section's conditions.** Nothing in E.1 through E.5 limits that treatment, and eligibility for it does not depend on ICHRA coverage or on the purchase channel conditions above.

**E.7 Tax consequences of error.** Employer acknowledges that a salary reduction not permitted under this Section produces Form W-2 and employment tax corrections and may constitute an operational failure of the cafeteria plan, and that those consequences are Employer's under Section 5.4 of the Agreement.

## F. No shopping, steering, or producer activity

**F.1 SSA is not a producer.** SSA is not a licensed insurance producer, agency, or broker with respect to individual health insurance coverage; **receives no commission or other compensation from any issuer or carrier**; and does not solicit, negotiate, sell, or place individual coverage.

**F.2 No recommendation or default.** SSA does not recommend, rank, curate, endorse, select, or establish a default with respect to any issuer, plan, or policy, and does not advise any employee as to which coverage to obtain, whether to obtain coverage, or whether to opt out of the ICHRA.

**F.3 No enrollment originates with SSA.** **No enrollment in individual health insurance coverage originates with SSA or through any SSA system.** Enrollment occurs with the issuer or through a licensed entity, and SSA is not a party to it.

**F.4 Reference information only.** Any market, plan, rate, or affordability information SSA furnishes is indicative reference information furnished to Employer or to Employer's licensed broker or agent of record; **is limited to coverage available outside an Exchange and is therefore incomplete by design**; is not a quotation, an offer, or advice; and is not furnished to any employee except as Employer and its licensed producer direct and on their responsibility.

**F.5 Third-party data.** Plan, rate, and coverage information is sourced from third parties. **SSA makes no warranty as to its accuracy, completeness, currency, or fitness for any purpose**, rates and availability are subject to change by issuers, and SSA is not liable for reliance on it.

**F.6 Employer's producer.** Employer's broker or agent of record is separately engaged by Employer, is not SSA's agent or subcontractor, and **SSA is not responsible for that person's licensure, conduct, suitability determinations, recommendations, or communications with Employer or any employee.** Employer indemnifies SSA for that conduct under Section 5.6 of the Agreement.

## G. Owners and individuals who cannot participate on a tax-favored basis

Employer determines which individuals cannot receive ICHRA reimbursements or cafeteria plan benefits on a tax-favored basis, including more-than-2% shareholders of an S corporation, partners, sole proprietors, and certain family members treated as owners. **SSA applies the tax treatment Employer directs and relies on Employer's determination of status.** Where Employer directs post-tax treatment, SSA administers the arrangement accordingly.

---

## H. Continuation, card, and funding

**H.1 Continuation.** Continuation of the ICHRA is governed by **APX-COBRA Section E**, including that the ICHRA rather than any individual policy is the continuable coverage, that Employer certifies the applicable premium, that substantiation continues, and that a qualified beneficiary who is not an active employee is not issued an electronic payment card.

**H.2 Card and funding.** Where the electronic payment card is elected, **APX-CARD** and **SCH-BANKING** govern card issuance, settlement, and the employer contribution and salary reduction amounts loaded to it. A missed or unfunded payroll results in no funding for the affected period and never in participant debt.

⚠️ **H.3 Premium payment failures.** Employer acknowledges that a declined or unfunded premium payment may result in the lapse of a participant's individual health insurance coverage, that a lapse may cause the individual not to be enrolled in individual coverage for the affected month, and that the consequences of such a lapse for the participant's ICHRA eligibility and tax treatment are determined by the ICHRA's terms and applicable law. **SSA does not advance premium and does not guarantee that any premium payment will be honored.** Employer's funding obligations under APX-PAYMENT and APX-CARD apply.

## I. Regulatory environment

Employer acknowledges that the regulatory framework governing individual coverage HRAs is of recent origin, has been the subject of litigation and proposed amendment, and is subject to change; that SSA's services are provided against guidance as it exists from time to time; and that **Section 4.2 of the Agreement permits SSA to revise fees where a change in law, regulation, guidance, or its interpretation materially revises the nature or scope of the services.**

## J. Termination

On termination, APX-RA Section E applies, and in addition: SSA ceases delivering notices and collecting substantiation and opt-out elections; any electronic payment card is deactivated; **no participant has any vested right in an unused ICHRA amount** except as the ICHRA's terms provide; and substantiation and notice records are delivered as provided in Section 3.5 of the Agreement.

## Notes to Kevin

### 1. What this Rider deliberately does not say

**No notice deadline.** Section C states that Employer sets the schedule and that SSA delivers on it, and states no date — not 90 days, not a newly-established-plan rule, not a new-hire rule. Your register records the ICHRA notice analysis as **open with no basis (LA-08)**, and separately records that an existing internal document asserts a newly-established-plan answer **with no citation anywhere**. A contract term is the worst possible place for that assertion to surface, because it becomes a commitment rather than a working assumption. If the research later settles it, the Rider does not need to change — only Employer's schedule does.

**No affordability figure to employees.** A.8 and F.4 confine any affordability output to Employer and its producer. ⚠️ **Separately and more importantly: A.8 is bracketed as a scope decision.** Your register flags a correctness dependency — if the lowest-cost silver plan input is drawn from off-Exchange-only data, an understated figure makes an unaffordable offer look affordable, and the determination carrying that error came from SSA. **My recommendation is to leave A.8 out of scope at launch** and add it by addendum once the data source is settled. Contracting for it now creates an obligation to furnish something you may not want to furnish yet.

### 2. Section E is the highest-risk drafting in the set

E.1 adopts the **restrictive reading** — salary reduction available only to a participant **covered by** the ICHRA, not merely offered one. Your register records that the "covered by" versus "offered" question has not been read against primary text and calls it the most load-bearing unverified item in the structure, with reversal cost low before any employee election is collected and high afterward.

Drafting to the restrictive reading is the cheap-to-reverse choice: if the permission turns out to be broader, the Rider is merely more conservative than required and loosening it is an amendment. If it had been drafted the other way and the restrictive reading is right, employees who waived would have taken impermissible pre-tax reductions, with per-employee per-pay-period corrections. **E.1 should not be loosened before that research is done.**

E.6 keeps excepted benefit premiums on a separate footing in their own paragraph, deliberately, so that the two rails are not read as one.

### 3. D.5 is a design decision with a consequence

D.5 says that contradictory coverage information overrides an attestation and holds reimbursement. That is the right answer, and it is also the clause that makes a policy-status data feed **binding** once it exists — your register notes that receiving such data may convert a passive reliance shield into an active monitoring obligation, and that the option of not knowing cannot be recovered once a feed is ingested.

D.5 as drafted is scoped to information SSA actually receives, not to information SSA could obtain, which is the narrowest version that still behaves correctly. Worth knowing you are committing to it.

### 4. B.7 is where the employer's own conduct does the work

The ERISA safe harbor turns on facts about Employer's behavior — endorsement, cash-in-lieu, consideration received. No contract clause makes those facts true. B.7 states the conditions, assigns them to Employer, and says plainly that SSA's services support them but Employer's conduct determines them. That is the honest allocation and it is also the one that protects you.

### 5. Decisions open

1. **A.8 affordability — in scope or out at launch.** My recommendation: out.
2. **Whether the ICHRA plan document will require third-party documentation rather than attestation** for the annual substantiation in D.1(a). Drafted to permit attestation, consistent with your registered position that attestation is sufficient and that its capture is the liability shield.
3. **Whether to name the cafeteria plan rail "PremiumPath" anywhere in the contract.** Drafted with no product name — the Rider describes the arrangement generically, which keeps it usable for any employer and any partner agency. Branding belongs in marketing and in the Fee Schedule, not in the operative terms.

**Superior State Administrators, Inc.**

## Recitals

Employer has requested Superior State Administrators, Inc., a Michigan corporation formerly doing business as Superior State Employer Solutions ("SSA"), to act as its agent for administrative services related to certain benefit programs and arrangements described in the Appendices to this Agreement. SSA provides those services as agent of Employer. In consideration of the mutual promises contained here, Employer and SSA agree as follows.

## ARTICLE I — INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Effective date, term, and renewal
This Agreement commences on **______________** and continues for an initial term of twelve (12) months, renewing automatically for successive twelve (12) month periods until terminated under Section 6.11. An Appendix or Schedule incorporated into this Agreement may carry a later effective date.

### 1.2 Structure of this Agreement
This Agreement consists of:

- these Articles I through VIII;
- the **Business Associate Agreement** (BAA-2026);
- each **Service Appendix** elected in Article VII;
- each **Schedule** referenced by an elected Appendix, including the Schedule of Banking Authorizations (SCH-BANKING) and the Designated Contact List (SCH-CONTACT); and
- the **Fee Schedule**, being the SSA fee schedule for the proposal identified in Article IV.

Each is incorporated by reference. Services apply only to the extent elected in Article VII and described in an elected Appendix.

### 1.3 Order of precedence
In the event of conflict:

1. A **Service Appendix** controls as to the description, scope, and performance of the services it covers.
2. These **Articles** control as to all other matters, including limitation of liability, indemnification, term, termination, dispute resolution, and governing law.
3. The **Business Associate Agreement** controls as to the use and disclosure of Protected Health Information, **subject to Article V's limitation of liability, which applies to it.**
4. The **Fee Schedule** controls as to fees.

### 1.4 Scope of undertaking
Employer has sole and final authority to control and manage the operation of its plans and arrangements (each, a "Plan"). SSA is and remains an independent contractor and is not for any purpose an employee of Employer; the parties are not partners and are not engaged in a joint venture. SSA does not assume responsibility for the design of any Plan, the adequacy of its funding, or any act, omission, or breach of duty by Employer. SSA is not an insurer, underwriter, or guarantor of any benefit. SSA provides administrative and reimbursement services only and assumes no financial risk or obligation for benefits payable by Employer under any Plan. Nothing here constitutes SSA a party to any Plan, confers on SSA any authority or control over Plan management or administration, or imposes on SSA any obligation to any employee or participant ("Participant").

### 1.5 Status of SSA
**SSA is never the plan administrator of any Plan.** SSA is not a fiduciary within the meaning of ERISA §3(21), is not a named fiduciary, is not the plan administrator within the meaning of ERISA §3(16), and is not a claims fiduciary. SSA exercises no discretionary authority or control over the administration of any Plan or over Plan assets. All duties SSA performs under this Agreement are **non-discretionary** and are performed under Employer's direction and against Employer's written Plan terms.

### 1.6 Funds
**Except as expressly provided in an elected Appendix, SSA does not hold, receive, take custody of, or take title to Employer funds, Plan funds, or Participant funds.** Where an Appendix provides otherwise — including the collection of continuation coverage premiums under the COBRA and Continuation Services Appendix — SSA acts solely as agent of Employer, holds such amounts for remittance to Employer as provided in that Appendix, and does not hold them in trust. **SSA is not a signatory on and has no independent access to any Employer account.**

### 1.7 No tax or legal advice
SSA does not provide tax or legal advice. Documents SSA prepares are drafts furnished for Employer's review **with Employer's own legal counsel**, and the decision to adopt, execute, and distribute any Plan document is Employer's alone. Employer is solely responsible for determining the legal and tax status of each Plan.

## ARTICLE II — EMPLOYER RESPONSIBILITIES

### 2.1 Sole authority
Employer has sole authority and responsibility for each Plan and its operation, including administering, construing, and interpreting Plan provisions and making all determinations under them. Employer authorizes SSA to act on its behalf only as expressly stated in this Agreement or as mutually agreed in writing. **All final determinations of a Participant's entitlement to benefits are made by Employer, including any determination on appeal of a denied claim.** Employer is the Plan Administrator and Named Fiduciary of each Plan for purposes of ERISA.

### 2.2 Employer duties
Without limiting Section 2.1, it is Employer's sole responsibility, as Plan Administrator, to: ensure compliance with COBRA; perform required nondiscrimination testing; amend each Plan as necessary for ongoing compliance; file required tax and governmental returns, including Form 5500 returns; determine whether a valid election change has occurred; handle Participant claim appeals; execute, distribute, and retain required Plan and claims documentation; and take all other steps necessary to maintain and operate each Plan in compliance with the Plan's terms, ERISA, HIPAA, the Internal Revenue Code, and other applicable federal and state law — **except to the extent SSA expressly assumes a duty in an elected Appendix.**

### 2.3 Reporting, disclosure, and attestation obligations
Employer is responsible for determining the applicability of, and for satisfying, all reporting, disclosure, attestation, and filing obligations applicable to its Plans, including without limitation: Forms 5500 and 720 (PCORI); Internal Revenue Code §6055 and §6056 reporting; Form W-2 reporting; Summary of Benefits and Coverage; prescription drug data reporting; the annual gag clause prohibition compliance attestation; transparency in coverage requirements; mental health parity nonquantitative treatment limitation comparative analyses; and any successor or replacement obligation. **SSA provides no assistance with these obligations except to the extent expressly assumed in an elected Appendix, and will furnish data in its possession as reasonably requested for an additional fee to be agreed.**

### 2.4 Information to SSA
Employer will furnish the information SSA determines necessary to perform its functions, including information concerning each Plan and the eligibility of individuals to participate and receive benefits, in the time and manner agreed. Employer will notify SSA of any change to a Plan at least thirty (30) days before its effective date.

### 2.5 Liability for claims
Employer is responsible for payment of claims made under, and the benefits provided by, each Plan. SSA does not insure or underwrite Employer's liability. Except for expenses SSA expressly assumes in this Agreement, Employer is responsible for all expenses incident to each Plan.

### 2.6 HIPAA privacy
Employer will provide SSA with its notice of privacy practices and any subsequent changes; with any change to or revocation of a Participant's permission to use or disclose PHI that affects SSA's permitted or required uses or disclosures; and with notice of any restriction on the use or disclosure of PHI that Employer has agreed to. Employer will not request SSA to use or disclose PHI in any manner that would not be permissible under HIPAA if done by Employer, except that SSA may use or disclose PHI for data aggregation and for SSA's own management and administrative activities as provided in the Business Associate Agreement.

### 3.6 Standard of care; erroneous payments

If SSA makes a payment to an ineligible person or in an incorrect amount, SSA will make a diligent effort to recover it, but is not liable for the payment unless it is otherwise liable under this Agreement.

### 3.7 Privacy and security
A separate Business Associate Agreement governs SSA's use and disclosure of PHI and is incorporated into and made a part of this Agreement. SSA maintains its own privacy and information security practices and will furnish a summary on Employer's reasonable request.

### 3.8 Subcontracting
SSA may subcontract any service to qualified vendors, including its benefits administration platform provider, card program provider, and data providers. SSA remains responsible for subcontracted performance under the Standard of Care and will require each subcontractor that receives PHI to agree to restrictions and conditions no less protective than those in the Business Associate Agreement.

### 3.9 Additional duties
The parties may agree in writing to additional duties, which will be specified in an Appendix or amendment.

### Decisions open

1. **§4.6 compensation disclosure.** Drafted to disclose your own direct compensation in full and to state you take no carrier compensation, leaving broker amounts to the broker's own disclosure. This is the position that is accurate under your model and does not publish an agent's markup — **but it does not resolve whether ERISA §408(b)(2)(B) reaches SSA itself**, which remains flagged from the review and is in tension with the project's standing "markup invisible to the employer" boundary. Your call whether to go further.
2. **§6.8 dispute resolution.** Option A drafted. Option B, if you prefer finality over court: binding AAA Commercial arbitration, single arbitrator, seat in Menominee, Michigan, award enforceable in any court of competent jurisdiction.
3. **§5.8 super-cap.** Bracketed. Expect a sophisticated employer's counsel to ask for a BAA carve-out; having the fallback drafted is cheaper than negotiating it live.
4. **Numbers to set:** §4.3 dispute window, §4.4 and §4.5 periods, §6.9 limitations period if one year is too aggressive for your market.

### Flags

1. ⚠️ **§1.6 is the clause I'd most want a second read on.** It is scoped rather than flat because COBRA premiums pass through SSA. "Holds for remittance to Employer, not in trust" is the honest description of the arrangement, and it is also the sentence that will be read closely in any state TPA licensing inquiry — the same fact already registered as satisfying prong 1 of the Texas definition. **APX-COBRA now needs a custody section** (account titling, non-interest-bearing or interest retained as compensation, remittance timing, NSF and reversal allocation, no advance of premium). Not drafted here.
2. ⚠️ **§1.5 says SSA is never the plan administrator, while APX-FSA and APX-HRA will give SSA initial claim determinations and up to a first-level appeal.** Those appendices must characterize that as a ministerial application of Employer's written Plan terms, with Employer holding final authority. The two statements are reconcilable; they have to be reconciled in the drafting rather than left to inference.
3. **§2.3's catch-all** avoids needing to resolve whether each CAA-era obligation reaches an account-based plan — the review's open VERIFY items 2 and 3. It allocates without adjudicating.
4. **Transit is now out of the cafeteria-plan recital.** APX-TRANSIT will not carry §125 or §105(h) testing, SPD, 5500, SBC, or COBRA language. That is a substantive narrowing of what a transit-only client currently signs.

## Notes to Kevin

1. **Why this is a schedule rather than a block in the agreement.** In the current document the contact list is three blank lines inside Article VI, which means a personnel change technically requires amending the agreement. Nobody does that, so in practice the list goes stale and the authorization behind every PHI disclosure is a signature from years ago. As a replaceable schedule it can be kept current by one page.
2. **Part 2 is the authorization that BAA §2.16 relies on.** That section limits your PHI disclosures to the plan sponsor to individuals on this list plus what §164.504(f) permits. Without a maintained list, §2.16 points at nothing.
3. **Part 3 exists to stop list inflation.** Billing and funding contacts have no reason to receive PHI, and today the only list available is the PHI list, so they end up on it. Separating them narrows your disclosure surface at no cost.
4. **Part 4 is the clause that protects you on a departed employee.** Removal is effective when you have had a reasonable opportunity to act, not retroactively.
5. **Five rows rather than three** in Part 2, with a scope column. ⚠️ Confirm the scope limiter is something you can actually enforce in the platform — if access is all-or-nothing per employer, drop the "Limited" checkbox rather than promising a granularity you cannot deliver.
6. **AdobeSign:** Part 2 and Part 3 rows should be repeatable or conditionally visible; all email fields validated; the supersession checkbox in the header should be required so a replacement is never ambiguous about what it replaces.

Types of Section 105 / HRA Plans

Traditional HRA

The Original Reimbursement Model

The employer sets aside a defined amount for each employee. Employees submit claims for reimbursement. Unused funds carry over year to year based on plan design — building a balance that encourages employees to become more thoughtful healthcare consumers.

Key Rules

  • Must integrate with a group health plan satisfying ACA requirements
  • Employer-funded only — no employee contributions
  • All employees treated uniformly (special rules for age and family size)
  • Cannot reimburse individual health insurance premiums
  • No IRS contribution limit — employer decides the amount
ICHRA

Individual Coverage HRA

Flips the traditional model — instead of offering group insurance and adding an HRA, the employer funds an HRA and employees purchase their own individual coverage. Employer reimburses premiums and optionally other qualified medical expenses.

Key Rules

  • Cannot offer group insurance and ICHRA to the same employee class
  • Employees may be divided into classes with different contribution amounts
  • Employees must be enrolled in individual health coverage
  • Medicare-enrolled employees can participate
  • No IRS limit on contributions
MERP

Medical Expense Reimbursement Plan

Built on the same Section 105 foundation as an HRA, but the strategy is fundamentally different. Cost-savings driven — a plan design tool that helps employers reduce overall health plan spending by taking on manageable, predictable risk. Premium savings of 40–50% are common.

Key Rules

  • No carryover — use-it-or-lose-it by design
  • Employee cost sharing possible via Section 125 cafeteria plan
  • Multiple benefit tiers possible from a single underlying policy
  • Integrates with high-deductible group plan
QSEHRA

Qualified Small Employer HRA

Created specifically for small employers who don't offer group insurance. A simple, compliant way to help employees pay for individual coverage and medical expenses.

Key Rules

  • 2026 IRS Limits: $6,450 self-only / $13,100 family
  • Fewer than 50 FT employees required
  • Cannot offer any group health plan
  • All eligible employees offered same terms
  • Written notice required 90 days before plan year
  • Reimbursements may reduce premium tax credit
EBHRA

Excepted Benefit HRA

Supplements — not replaces — a group health plan. Covers excepted benefits like dental, vision, COBRA continuation, and short-term insurance. Employees don't have to be enrolled in the group plan to participate.

Key Rules

  • 2026 IRS Limit: $2,200 per employee
  • Must offer group plan; enrollment not required for EBHRA eligibility
  • Cannot reimburse major medical premiums or Medicare
  • Carryover allowed — doesn't count against next year's limit
  • Does not disqualify HSA eligibility

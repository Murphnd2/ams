What Employers Need to Know

Plan year and elections. FSAs operate on a 12-month plan year. Employees elect during open enrollment; the amount is deducted evenly from each paycheck. Elections can't change mid-year unless a qualifying life event occurs (marriage, birth/adoption, loss of coverage, etc.).

Carryover vs. grace period. The "use it or lose it" fear is the single biggest barrier to participation. Employers now have two options:

Carryover — Employees carry over up to $680 of unused Healthcare FSA or LPFSA funds. Most popular option. Grace period — Extra 2½ months to incur expenses. A plan can offer one or the other, not both.

Nondiscrimination testing. IRS requires FSA benefits not favor highly compensated employees. Superior State performs annual testing and works with you on corrections before they become a problem.

Real Tax Savings — For Both Sides of the Paycheck

For employees: FSA contributions are exempt from federal income tax, state income tax, Social Security, and Medicare — a combined savings of roughly 20–30% on every dollar. An employee in the 22% bracket contributing $2,500 saves approximately $740 in taxes.

For employers: Every pre-tax dollar reduces your FICA/Medicare matching by 7.65%. Across 25 employees averaging $1,800 in contributions, that's roughly $3,400/year in payroll tax savings — more than enough to cover plan administration.

Summit Card — Convenient Access to FSA Funds

Employees pay for eligible expenses at the point of sale with the Summit Card benefit debit card — no out-of-pocket waiting. The card is tied directly to the FSA balance, and eligible transactions are automatically matched. Superior State handles card setup, replenishment, monitoring, and substantiation.
